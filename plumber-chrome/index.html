Chrome is the part of Carteret that grew up around the steel and chemical plants of the early 1900s. Wikipedia describes it as an unincorporated community inside the borough, in the southeast, with Roosevelt Avenue (County Route 602) running through it. This page is for people who live or own property there and want to know what to call about a drain, a water heater or a leak.

We found no published map that draws a hard line around Chrome, so we describe it by its anchor street and not by exact blocks. If your address is on or near Roosevelt Avenue in the southeast of the borough and you are unsure whether it counts as Chrome, call and give us the address. We will tell you whether we can help.

Chrome at a glance

Where it sits.

Southeast Carteret, with Roosevelt Avenue (County Route 602) running through it. Boundaries are not published, so the avenue is our anchor.

Where the name comes from.

Local history credits the Chrome Steel, Liebig and U.S. Metals and Refining plants and the jobs they brought after about 1905.

Housing mix.

A data site that profiles the Chrome census tract reports that about half of its buildings are small 2, 3 or 4 unit apartment buildings.

Plumbing focus.

Shared drains and shutoffs in small multi-unit buildings, plus ordinary water heater, fixture and leak problems.

Before you call about a problem in Chrome

  1. Find your shutoff.

    Close the valve for your unit or for the building. In a small multi-unit building the valves may sit in a basement or utility closet, so ask the owner where they are.

  2. Tell the owner or manager.

    If you rent, report the problem in writing as soon as you see it. Equipment such as the water heater usually belongs to the owner.

  3. Protect what you can.

    Lift boxes and electronics off a wet floor. Stay away from outlets and the panel if the floor is wet.

  4. Note what you see.

    Write down which fixtures are slow, where the water appears and when it started. Then call (718) 412-8453.

What is known about housing in Chrome

A data site that profiles the census tract labeled Chrome says about 51 percent of the real estate there is small apartment buildings of two to four units. It also says many homes date from 1940 to 1969 and some are older. Treat those figures as a rough description from one source, not a survey of every block.

We do not know the age or pipe material of any particular building, and a neighborhood profile cannot tell you. That depends on each building's repairs and renovations. A camera look inside the drain, as described on our sewer camera inspection page, shows what is really under the floor.

Verified local facts about Chrome

These points come from public sources listed at the end of this box. We have kept to what the sources actually say.

  • Wikipedia lists Chrome as an unincorporated community within Carteret, with a listed elevation of 13 feet.
  • The name came from the steel, chemical and metals plants that opened in the area around 1905 and brought a fast rise in population, according to the Carteret Historical Committee.
  • The Chrome main street was once a busy shopping district, and Roosevelt Avenue still runs through it.
  • Borough tenure matters here. Census QuickFacts puts the owner-occupied rate for the whole borough at 54.7 percent, so roughly 45 percent of homes are rented.

Sources: Wikipedia: Chrome, New Jersey. Carteret Historical Committee. NeighborhoodScout: Chrome. ROI-NJ: Chrome Waterfront RFP. Census QuickFacts: Carteret.

Tenants, owners and who pays

Because so many Carteret homes are rented, a lot of plumbing problems start with a tenant who is unsure who pays. The short answer depends on the lease and on what failed. Report the problem to the owner or manager in writing, keep dated photos and avoid repairs that could be disputed later.

When we are called to a building with several tenants, we explain the cause to the tenant and the owner in the same plain terms. A clog that serves several units is traced first, because the fix is different from a single stopped sink.

The Chrome waterfront redevelopment area

The borough has a Chrome Waterfront Redevelopment Area. In 2024 it published a request for qualifications and proposals for a 6.986 acre parcel there, Block 304 Lot 4, and said the use would follow the area's redevelopment plan. We found no published housing plan for that parcel, so we do not describe one.

The practical point for nearby owners is small. If water service on your street is interrupted for any work, your supplier is Middlesex Water Company, and its customer service line is 800-549-3802. After a shutoff, run a cold tap until the water clears before using hot water appliances.

Chrome plumbing questions

Is Chrome a separate town from Carteret?

No. Chrome is an unincorporated community inside Carteret borough in Middlesex County. Borough rules, permits and inspections apply, and the borough uses ZIP code 07008.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Chrome?

Permit rules vary, so ask the Carteret Building, Construction and Land Use Department at 61 Cooke Avenue before work starts. That applies whether we do the job or anyone else does.

Several apartments in my building have slow drains. Who do I call?

Tell the owner or manager first, because a shared line is normally the owner's responsibility. A plumber can then clear the line and, if it keeps returning, look inside it with a camera. See sewer line cleaning.

Who supplies water in Chrome?

Middlesex Water Company supplies water in Carteret. Its customer service line is 800-549-3802. Plumbing on the property side of the meter is usually the owner's job to maintain.

How do I check a plumber's license?

New Jersey licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the Division of Consumer Affairs. Use the Division's online license lookup for any plumber before work begins.

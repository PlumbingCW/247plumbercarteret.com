How Long Does It Take to Install a Garbage Disposal in Carteret, NJ?

By the Carteret 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026. Updated October 24, 2026.

Swapping one garbage disposal for another usually takes about an hour to two hours when the old mounting hardware, power connection and drain pipe line up with the new unit. A first-time installation in a sink that never had one takes longer, because it needs an electrical connection, a dishwasher knockout and sometimes a change to the drain pipe. That is the honest answer for a Carteret, NJ kitchen.

Below is a step-by-step timeline, a table of what adds time, and a few points for renters and landlords. If your disposal is leaking under the sink right now, shut the cold water feed to the sink, switch the disposal off at the wall and call our 24/7 line.

The short answer, by type of job

A direct replacement is the quickest job. The plumber disconnects the old unit, loosens the mounting ring, fits the new unit onto the same flange and reconnects the drain and power. If everything matches, it is a short visit.

A new installation adds electrical work and drain changes, and a switch or outlet may need to be added. Those steps are why the same-looking job can take much longer in one kitchen than in another.

  • Direct swap of a similar unit: often one to two hours.
  • Upgrading to a larger unit in the same location: a bit longer if fittings differ.
  • First-time installation with a new outlet and drain changes: longer, and may need an electrician.
  • Dishwasher hookup: the knockout plug has to be removed or the dishwasher will not drain.

What happens during the visit

Knowing the order helps you plan the day and spot anything out of the ordinary.

  1. Shut off power and water.

    The unit is unplugged or the breaker is switched off, and the cold feed to the sink is closed.

  2. Remove the old unit.

    The drain trap and the dishwasher hose come off, and the mounting ring is loosened and the old unit lifted out.

  3. Mount and connect the new one.

    The sink flange is re-sealed, the new unit is mounted and the discharge pipe and trap are reconnected.

  4. Test.

    We run water, check for leaks at every joint, and run the unit to confirm it grinds and drains.

Is the disposal the problem, or the drain?

A jammed or humming disposal is a repair, not a replacement. A hum usually means the blades are stuck, and many units have a hex socket underneath for turning them by hand. A tripped reset button on the bottom is another quick check. Both need the power switched off first.

If water backs up into the sink when the disposal runs, the blockage may be in the drain beyond it. Our drain cleaning team can clear the line, and a repeated pattern can mean grease buildup that needs hydro jetting rather than a new disposal.

  • Humming and not turning: jammed blades.
  • Silent and dead: reset button or power.
  • Leaking from the bottom: seals have failed, replace.
  • Water backing up: drain blockage beyond the disposal.

Choosing the right unit before the visit

A larger horsepower rating grinds faster and jams less, but it also needs more room under the sink and a stronger mount. Ask whether your cabinet, drain and electrical supply suit the unit before you buy one online.

If you have a septic tank or an unusual drain, say so when you call. We also tell customers honestly when their existing disposal is cheaper to repair through our garbage disposal repair service than to replace.

Habits that make a disposal last

Run cold water before, during and for several seconds after grinding, so food scraps move through the trap rather than settling. Feed small amounts at a time, and keep grease, coffee grounds in bulk, pasta and fibrous scraps out of the unit.

Never put your hand in a disposal. If something is stuck, switch the power off at the breaker, not only the wall switch, and use tongs or pliers.

Renters and landlords in Carteret

Roughly 45 percent of Carteret homes are rented, so many installations involve a landlord's permission. If you are a tenant, ask before you buy or install a unit, because the appliance and any resulting leak or wiring problem may fall to the owner.

Landlords can save time by sending photos of the existing unit and the cabinet before the visit. We give both parties the same plain description of what we did, so there is no argument about the work afterward.

Why grease and food scraps matter more in Carteret's older sewer system

Carteret's sewer system has a long record of strain. A state sewer ban on West Carteret lasted 25 years and was lifted June 26, 2009, because old lines carried both sanitary sewage and stormwater and overloaded the pump stations in heavy rain.

The borough has also been rehabilitating the Roosevelt Avenue and Bergen Street pump stations with a $4.8 million NJ Water Bank loan closed in October 2020. That history is a good reason to keep grease and large scraps out of your drain, because a kitchen line that clogs often can back up into the basement or the street's shared line.

A disposal grinds food finer but does not stop grease from coating the pipe. If your kitchen drain slows within months of any new installation, tell the plumber before the next cleaning.

Code and safety references for this topic

Installing a disposal follows the National Standard Plumbing Code that New Jersey adopts through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the electrical connection is a separate trade. Cut power at the breaker before touching a disposal.

Questions readers ask

Can I install a garbage disposal myself?

Handy homeowners do, but the electrical connection and the leak-free seal are where most mistakes happen. If there is no outlet under the sink, call a licensed professional.

Do I need a permit to install a garbage disposal?

Permit rules vary, so ask the Carteret construction office or your plumber how the work will be handled. The borough's Building, Construction and Land Use Department is at 61 Cooke Avenue.

Why does the sink drain slowly after I put in a new disposal?

A new unit does not clear an existing drain blockage. If the line was already narrowed by grease, it needs cleaning, and Port Reading and Avenel customers with older kitchen lines often find that the cleaning is what actually fixed the slowdown.

Can you install a disposal the same day in Carteret?

We book same-day appointments when available. For a leaking or dead unit, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Is it a problem to use a disposal on an older Carteret sewer line?

Not by itself, but older lines are less forgiving of grease and fibrous scraps. Keep grease out of the sink and flush with plenty of cold water.

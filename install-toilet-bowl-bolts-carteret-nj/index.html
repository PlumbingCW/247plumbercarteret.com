How to Install Toilet Bowl Bolts in Carteret, NJ

By the Carteret 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026. Updated October 24, 2026.

To install toilet bowl bolts, set the new closet bolts into the slots of the floor flange, lift the toilet over them on a fresh wax ring, press it down evenly, then add washers and nuts and tighten a little at a time. The goal is a toilet that does not rock and a wax seal that does not leak.

This guide is for Carteret, NJ homeowners who have a loose toilet, a broken bolt cap or a rocking bowl and want to know whether the job is a weekend fix. We cover the tools, the steps, the mistakes that crack porcelain, and the cases where the flange itself is the real problem.

What you need before you start

This is a modest job, but it goes better with every item ready. You will be moving a heavy fixture, so a helper makes it safer. Get the new parts first, because an old wax ring cannot be reused once the toilet is lifted.

  • A new pair of closet bolts, with matching washers and nuts.
  • A new wax ring, or a wax-free seal if you prefer.
  • An adjustable wrench and a putty knife for the old wax.
  • A sponge and a bucket for the water left in the tank and bowl.
  • Plastic shims for an uneven floor, and old towels.

Step by step: replacing closet bolts and resetting the toilet

Work slowly. Porcelain is hard but brittle, and too much force on a nut is the usual cause of a crack.

  1. Shut off and drain.

    Close the shutoff valve, flush, hold the handle down and sponge out the rest. Disconnect the supply line.

  2. Remove the old toilet.

    Take off the bolt caps and nuts, rock the bowl gently and lift straight up. Set it on towels.

  3. Clean the flange and set new bolts.

    Scrape off old wax, check that the flange is solid, and slide the new bolts into the slots so they stand upright.

  4. Place the new wax ring and set the bowl.

    Lower the toilet straight down over the bolts and press evenly. Do not twist it once the wax touches.

  5. Tighten gradually.

    Add washers and nuts, then tighten each side a little at a time. Stop when the toilet is firm and does not rock.

Problems that look like a bolt issue but are not

A toilet that moves does not always need new bolts. Use this guide to avoid replacing parts that were never the cause.

Common mistakes that turn a small job into a bigger one

The most frequent mistake is overtightening. One side gets cranked down while the other is loose, and the porcelain flexes until it cracks. Alternate sides in small turns and stop as soon as the toilet is steady.

The second mistake is reusing the wax ring or setting the toilet down, lifting it and setting it again. Wax seals on the first contact, and a second try can leave a gap that leaks slowly into the subfloor. If you must lift the toilet again, use a new ring.

When the flange or the floor is the real problem

If the new bolts will not hold, the flange may be cracked, rusted or loose on a rotted subfloor. That is not a bolt job. The flange has to be repaired, and sometimes the floor under it has to be rebuilt.

Our toilet repair team resets, reseals and repairs flanges, and we can tell you whether the toilet itself is still worth keeping. If water has already reached the ceiling below, treat it as a leak and call the 24/7 line.

  • Old rust or a visibly broken flange: repair before reinstalling.
  • Soft or spongy floor near the toilet: moisture damage, stop and call.
  • A toilet that keeps running after the reset: a fill valve or flapper issue, not the bolts.

After the reset: how to check your work

Open the shutoff valve, let the tank fill and flush several times. Look at the base and the supply connection for moisture, and press a paper towel around the bottom for a minute. Check again the next day, because slow wax leaks take time to show.

If you find a drip at the tank or supply connection, our faucet and sink repair crew carries the fittings and can fix the supply side. Cap the bolts last, once you are sure the toilet is dry and stable.

Choosing the right bolts and washers

Closet bolts come in brass, stainless steel and plated steel. Stainless and brass resist rust, which makes the next repair easier, and plated steel can seize after a few years in a damp bathroom. Choose a length that leaves a short stub above the nut, because a bolt that is too long will not fit under the cap.

Use the plastic or metal washers supplied with the set, and keep them flat against the base of the toilet. A bent or missing washer lets the nut bite into the porcelain and is a quiet cause of cracks.

Water shutoffs and rentals: what is different in Carteret

Carteret's water comes from Middlesex Water Company, which has been replacing mains, valves and service lines in the borough under its RENEW program. During a service line transfer, customers could be without water for six to eight hours, with advance notice. That is a reminder to test your toilet shutoff valve before you need it.

A shutoff valve that will not close is the most common reason a simple bolt job turns into a plumber visit. If yours is stuck, shut off the main instead and plan to replace the valve at the same time.

Many Carteret homes are rented, about 45 percent, and in a rental the toilet usually belongs to the owner. Tell the landlord about a rocking or leaking toilet before attempting a repair, so you do not take on the cost of a water damage claim.

Code and safety references for this topic

Setting a toilet follows the National Standard Plumbing Code that New Jersey adopts through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Permit rules vary, so ask the borough construction office if the project moves the drain.

Questions readers ask

Do I need to replace the wax ring every time I move the toilet?

Yes. A used wax ring is compressed and may not seal a second time. A new ring costs little compared with water damage under the floor.

How tight should toilet bolts be?

Snug, then a bit more, tightened evenly. Stop when the toilet no longer rocks. Cranking down is the most common way to crack the bowl.

Why does my toilet still rock after new bolts?

The floor may be uneven or the flange may sit too low. Plastic shims fix a small gap, and a low flange needs a spacer or repair.

Can I use the same bolts as before?

Old bolts are often rusted or bent, and replacements are inexpensive. New stainless or brass bolts are easier to remove later.

Can a Carteret rental tenant replace toilet bolts without asking?

It is better to tell the landlord first. In many leases the fixture belongs to the owner, and a written note protects you if a leak shows up later.

Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Carteret, NJ

Same-day tank swaps when a heater has failed, with clear pricing before the old unit comes out.

Call (718) 412-8453See all services

Water heater replacement in Carteret, NJ means draining the failed tank, setting a new one and reconnecting water, gas or power, venting and safety valves so it runs properly from the first hour. When a tank is flooding a basement or has left a family cold overnight, our 24/7 emergency line takes the call and we book a same-day swap whenever we can.

Most replacements are forced, not planned. The tank gives out, the floor gets wet and the decision has to be made quickly. This page explains how we handle that, what choices you have and what to ask so the new heater fits your house and your budget.

Tank flooding the floor or no hot water at all: act now

Treat a heater that is pouring water as an active emergency. The faster the supply is closed, the less damage there is to drywall, boxes and flooring, and the sooner we can start work.

  • Shut the cold water valve at the top of the tank, then the main shutoff if the valve will not close.
  • Turn off the breaker for an electric heater or the gas control for a gas heater.
  • Gas smell: leave, move at least 350 feet away and call PSE&G at 1-800-880-PSEG (7734) or 911 before calling us.
  • Photograph the data plate and the leak. Those two pictures speed up the quote and help with an insurance claim.

What happens on a replacement day

A good swap is mostly preparation. We lay out each stage so you can plan your morning around it.

  1. Confirm the failure.

    We look at the tank, the fittings and the age on the data plate, and tell you if a cheaper repair would be enough.

  2. Agree the unit and price.

    You choose size and fuel type, and we state the price for the unit, labor, haul-away and new parts before starting.

  3. Drain, disconnect and remove.

    We drain the old tank, disconnect water, gas or power and carry it out.

  4. Set, connect and test.

    The new tank goes in with new connectors, a relief valve and proper venting, then we fire it, check for leaks and measure the outlet temperature.

Choosing the right replacement

Matching the new heater to your household avoids running out of hot water or paying to heat water you never use.

Sizing the new tank for how you use hot water

Bigger is not always better. A tank that is too large costs more to heat all day. One that is too small leaves the last shower cold. We ask how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have and whether laundry and dishwashers run at the same time.

A two-person apartment and a house with teenagers and a laundry room need different answers. If your old tank always ran short, say so, because the original may simply have been too small.

Permits, old basements and what Carteret requires

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code under N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and Carteret's Building, Construction and Land Use Department at 61 Cooke Avenue reviews and inspects permitted plumbing work. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the borough office or let us explain how your swap will be handled.

Carteret flooded badly during Hurricane Sandy in October 2012, and parts of town sit close to the Arthur Kill and the Rahway River. If your heater is in a low basement, we can raise it off the floor on a stand when we replace it, which protects the burner or elements from a small spill.

Check the pressure-relief discharge pipe too. It should end where escaping hot water cannot hurt anyone and where it is easy to see.

Why replacement beats one more repair

A leaking tank body cannot be patched, and a heater near ten years old with several worn parts is likely to fail again. Paying for a repair that lasts a few months wastes money and leaves you open to a midnight failure.

If you are not sure whether the tank is finished, our water heater repair team will tell you honestly. We would rather fix a part than sell a tank.

  • Water coming from the tank body, not a fitting.
  • Rust-colored hot water on every tap.
  • A second major repair on a tank that is already old.
  • Repeated burner or element failures.

What drives the cost of a replacement

You get a single clear price before work starts, covering the unit, labor and disposal. The things that change it:

  • Tank size and typeA standard tank, a heat pump unit and a tankless conversion are very different products.
  • Fuel and ventingA gas flue that needs rerouting or a new liner adds parts and labor.
  • Code itemsExpansion tanks, relief valves, seismic or support items and permits depend on the job.
  • Access and removalStairs, tight turns and finished spaces make the old tank harder to carry out.
  • Time of callNights, weekends and holidays are emergency visits and are priced as such, with the number given first.

Some homeowners policies pay for resulting water damage but not the heater itself, so ask your insurer. For your exact number, call (718) 412-8453.

Before you buy: questions worth asking any plumber

Ask whether the quote lists the unit, the labor, haul-away, new connectors and a relief valve. Ask what the manufacturer and the labor warranties cover. Ask who handles the permit and whether the vent needs changes.

Ask us the same questions. If your new unit is a water heater installation in a new location, such as moving it out of a damp corner, mention that early, because it changes the job.

What a good replacement leaves behind

When the job is done, the new heater should be level, labeled with its install date and fitted with shutoff valves you can actually reach. We show you where the cold supply valve, the breaker or gas control and the relief discharge are, so the next emergency starts with the right hand on the right valve.

We also set the thermostat to a sensible temperature and explain the setting. A heater run too hot wastes fuel and raises the chance of a scald, while one set too low can leave the household short on a cold morning. Keep your paperwork and the warranty card together in one place.

Water Heater Replacement questions from Carteret customers

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Carteret?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered nights, weekends and holidays, and we book same-day replacements when stock and schedule allow. Tell us the size of your old tank and the fuel type so we can bring the right unit.

How long does a water heater replacement take?

A like-for-like swap often takes a few hours once the unit is on site. Venting changes, a new location or older piping can add time, and we tell you before we begin.

Should I replace a ten-year-old heater before it fails?

If it shows rust, moisture at the base or repeated repairs, planning a swap on your schedule is cheaper than an emergency. A heater with no symptoms can keep going, but you should know its age.

Can I switch from a gas tank to an electric or heat pump unit?

Often yes, but it depends on your panel capacity, available space and venting. We check those items before quoting so the change does not surprise you later.

Do you remove the old water heater?

Yes. Haul-away is part of the quote, and we drain and carry out the old tank so you are not left with it in the basement.

Does the borough need to approve the replacement?

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the borough construction office or let us explain how your replacement will be handled. We follow the plumbing subcode that applies to your address.

How do I tell the age of my heater?

The serial number on the data plate carries the date, though the format differs by brand. Send us a photo of the plate and we can usually decode it.

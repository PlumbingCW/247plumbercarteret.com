Commercial plumbing emergency: what to do first

A flooding restroom or an overflowing floor drain can close a business for the day. These steps protect the premises, customers and your insurance claim while a plumber is on the way, including calls overnight or on weekends.

  • Locate and close the nearest shutoff valve, or the main valve where the water enters the building.
  • Stop using the affected restrooms, sinks and floor drains. Put out wet-floor signs and keep staff and customers clear.
  • If water is near electrical panels or equipment, switch off those circuits from a dry location only.
  • Photograph the water, the source and any damaged stock or equipment before cleanup begins.

Kitchens, grease and the drains that fail first

Restaurants and food service sites lose the most hours to drain trouble. Grease from fryers, pasta water and kitchen scraps build up in floor drains and sink lines, and a grease trap that has not been emptied pushes everything toward the drain. A floor drain that gurgles before the dinner rush is usually a warning.

We clear the line with a cable or hydro jetting, check where the grease is accumulating and describe what kind of cleaning schedule would suit your volume.

  • Floor drains that back up at the same time every day: line or trap overloaded.
  • Slow hand sinks with a cold or sour smell: partial blockage in the branch.
  • Standing water around a dishwasher: drain hose or line problem.
  • Gurgling at floor drains when sinks run: venting or main line blockage.

Backflow and code questions for business owners

Commercial buildings often have to protect the public water supply from backflow. New Jersey's plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code, and backflow containment follows the Safe Drinking Water Act. In practice that means certain connections need a protective device.

Whether your property needs a device, a test or a replacement depends on how the water is used. The borough's Building, Construction and Land Use Department at 61 Cooke Avenue reviews permitted work, and permit rules vary, so ask the construction office or us before you start. We do not guess at code, and we will tell you plainly when something is a question for the borough.

Water main work in Port Reading and what it can do to your plumbing

Middlesex Water Company's 2023 RENEW program replaced about 26,000 feet of main across Carteret and part of Port Reading, with new service lines, valves and hydrants. The Port Reading streets on the list were Dawn Drive, Henry Street, Holly Street, Larch Street, Southeast Grant Avenue and Vernon Way.

If you live on one of those streets, the connection between the street main and your house was reworked. Lead and galvanized steel service lines found during the work were replaced with copper, including the customer-owned portion, at no direct cost to the owner or tenant. That is good news, but crews also shut the water for six to eight hours during the transfer, and sediment can loosen inside household pipes afterward.

After any water main job, we suggest running cold taps for a few minutes and then cleaning the small screens (aerators) on your faucets. If a faucet still sputters or a toilet fill valve runs, that debris is the usual cause and a faucet and sink repair clears it quickly.

Sources: Middlesex Water Company: $11.1 million RENEW project in Carteret and Port Reading.

Low ground, the tidal Arthur Kill and your basement

Port Reading faces the tidal Arthur Kill and sits at about 13 feet of elevation, much lower than Avenel or Fords. Water that has nowhere to go will head for the lowest point, which in many homes is the basement floor drain or the sump pit.

A sump pump that has sat idle all summer often fails on the first big storm. We test the float, the check valve and the discharge line, and we tell you when it is time to replace the pump rather than hope it holds. If water is already in the basement, shut off power to that area first and call the 24/7 line.

  • Test your pump before storms by lifting the float or pouring a bucket of water into the pit.
  • Keep the discharge pipe clear of leaves and make sure it ends away from the foundation.
  • Leave the floor drain uncovered and free of stored boxes so you notice a backup early.

Service lines, meters and what Middlesex Water does and does not own

Middlesex Water is also relocating customer meters from inside homes to exterior below-ground meter pits over time, usually when a meter comes up for testing. That change moves the utility's connection point outside, but the pipe from the pit to your house is still yours to maintain.

If your meter keeps spinning with every fixture off, the leak could be on that private side. We can check the line and, if it is leaking, advise whether a spot fix or a full water service line repair makes sense. For suspected hidden leaks inside the house, our leak detection service finds the source without opening more wall than needed.

Is emergency service available in Port Reading?

Will my plumbing be affected by the Middlesex Water main replacement?

It can be, for a short time. Expect a six to eight hour water shutoff while your service line is connected to the new main, then some sediment in the pipes. Run cold taps, clean faucet aerators, and call us if pressure drops or a fixture keeps running.

Is the lead service line replacement really free?

For lines found during the program, Middlesex Water says it replaces lead and galvanized steel service lines with copper at no direct cost to the owner or tenant, including the customer-owned section. Call Middlesex Water to confirm your own address, because it depends on where the work reaches.

What if my basement floods during a storm?

Shut off power to the flooded area if you can do it safely, stay out of standing water and call us. We check the sump pump and drains, and we explain whether the problem is a failed pump, a blocked line or ground water.

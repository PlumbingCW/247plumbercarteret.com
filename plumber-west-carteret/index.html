Plumber in West Carteret, NJ

Plumbing help for West Carteret, the part of the borough west of the Turnpike, with local facts about the sewer ban that ended in 2009.

West Carteret is the part of the borough that lies west of the New Jersey Turnpike. Roosevelt Avenue, the borough's main commercial street, runs through it, and Joseph Medwick Park gives it a greenway along the Rahway River. This page covers plumbing for homes there and spends time on one local fact no other part of Carteret shares, a state sewer ban that lasted about 25 years.

A sewer ban is a story about the public system, not about the pipes in any one house. It still helps explain why drain work here deserves care, and it gives homeowners two practical checks. Both are below.

West Carteret at a glance

Where it sits.

West of the NJ Turnpike, with Roosevelt Avenue (County Route 602) and Joseph Medwick Park along the Rahway River.

Housing.

A home data site lists about 410 homes, a median build year of 1954 and Cape Cods as common. Treat the figures as approximate.

Ownership.

A second data site describes the area as mostly owner occupied, with many homes built from 1940 to 1969.

Sewer history.

A state sewer ban ended on June 26, 2009 after years of pump station and pipe work.

Before you call about a problem in West Carteret

  1. Stop the water source.

    Close the shutoff valve on the line that is leaking. If you cannot tell which, close the main where the water line enters the house.

  2. Check the basement safely.

    Do not step into standing water near outlets, a panel or a plugged-in pump. Switch off power only from dry ground.

  3. Look for the source.

    Note whether water is coming from a pipe, a floor drain, the sump pit or the walls. Rain pointing to the sump or drain is useful to know.

  4. Call with details.

    Dial (718) 412-8453 and describe what you see, what you shut off and where the water is going.

What the West Carteret sewer ban was

The state environmental agency, NJDEP, put a sewer ban on West Carteret that lasted about 25 years. It was partly lifted for the rest of the borough in 1993 and ended for West Carteret on June 26, 2009. The borough said the old sewer system was over capacity, in places about 75 years old, and that stormwater and sewage lines were interconnected, so heavy rain overloaded the pump stations.

  • A 2001 study covered about 42,000 feet of sewer line, ended 102 illegal connections and found 57 more.
  • New or upgraded pump stations included Hayward Street and Dorothy Street.
  • About $5 million went to West Carteret improvements, plus $1 million from private developers.
  • NJDEP approved a report in May 2009 finding that key West Carteret areas met conveyance capacity standards.

Sources: Borough of Carteret: 25 year sewer ban lifted. Wikipedia: Carteret, New Jersey. Homes.com: West Carteret. NeighborhoodScout: West Carteret.

What that history means for your own house

An illegal connection in a sewer study usually means a roof drain, yard drain or sump pump that sends stormwater into the sanitary line. That is why the ban mattered to homeowners. It does not mean any particular house has bad pipes. The pipes in your house depend on your house, and a camera look under the floor is the only reliable way to know.

Two checks are worth doing before storm season, and the box below sets them out.

Two checks for a West Carteret basement

Neither check needs tools. Both take a few minutes and can prevent a wet basement.

  • Find where your sump pump discharges. It should send water outside and away from the foundation, not into a drain that joins the sanitary line. Rules vary, so ask the borough construction office if you are unsure.
  • Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and confirm the pump starts, empties the pit and stops. If it does not, see sump pump repair.

Living near the river and the Turnpike

Joseph Medwick Park runs along the Rahway River, so some West Carteret homes are near water. Whether a particular address is in a flood zone is a question for the FEMA flood map and your insurance records. Neither we nor a neighborhood profile can answer it for you.

The borough's Hurricane Sandy damage report, dated November 29, 2012, named the Public Works building and Waterfront Park, both on the Arthur Kill side of town. It reported about 200 homes seriously damaged across the borough. We do not claim West Carteret flooded in that storm.

Roosevelt Avenue businesses

Borough officials said lifting the ban would help the West Carteret commercial corridor grow again. Shops and restaurants along Roosevelt Avenue have different needs from homes, such as grease control and larger water heaters. Our commercial plumbing page covers that work.

Neighboring areas have their own guides. Avenel and Woodbridge Township border West Carteret, and Chrome and Downtown Carteret lie to the east.

West Carteret plumbing questions

Is the West Carteret sewer ban still in effect?

No. NJDEP lifted it on June 26, 2009, according to the borough, after the pump stations were upgraded and illegal connections were removed.

Does the old sewer ban mean my house has bad pipes?

No. The ban was about the capacity of the public system. The pipes inside and under your house depend on that house. A camera inspection shows their real condition.

Where should my sump pump discharge?

Outside, onto a path that carries stormwater away from your foundation. It should not feed the sanitary sewer. Rules differ by town, so confirm with the borough construction office at 61 Cooke Avenue.

Who do I report a street-wide sewer backup to?

Report it to the borough as well as to a plumber. A backup affecting several homes may be in the public line, which a plumber cannot repair from your property.

Which part of Carteret counts as West Carteret?

Wikipedia describes it as the part of the borough west of the NJ Turnpike. Exact boundaries are not published, so call and give your address if you are unsure.

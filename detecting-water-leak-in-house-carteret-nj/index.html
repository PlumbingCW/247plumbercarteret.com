Detecting a Water Leak in Your House: A Carteret, NJ Homeowner Guide

By the Carteret 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026. Updated October 24, 2026.

The fastest way to detect a water leak in your house is the meter test: turn off every fixture and appliance, then check whether the meter dial still moves. If it does, water is escaping somewhere. In a Carteret, NJ home, you can narrow the location with a few free checks before you pay for anyone to find it.

This guide walks through the checks in order, from the meter to the toilets to the walls, and explains when to stop and call. If water is actively flooding or spraying, shut the main valve and call our 24/7 line right away.

A five-minute test to confirm a leak

Do this when no one is using water, such as first thing in the morning or when everyone is out.

  1. Turn everything off.

    Stop the washer, dishwasher, ice maker and irrigation, and make sure no one flushes.

  2. Read the meter.

    Find the meter and note the leak indicator, usually a small dial or triangle, and the reading.

  3. Wait and look again.

    Wait fifteen to thirty minutes without using water, then check again.

  4. Interpret.

    If the dial moved or the reading changed, you have a leak somewhere.

Where leaks hide and how to check

Once you know there is a leak, work through the likely sources from the easiest to the hardest.

A wet spot is where the water shows up, not always where it escapes.

The toilet test, the cheapest check in the house

Toilets are the most common hidden leak. A flapper that does not seal lets water trickle from the tank into the bowl, and you may not hear it. Add a little food coloring to the tank and wait twenty minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper or fill valve is leaking.

Our toilet repair team can replace those parts quickly. A running toilet can waste a lot of water, so it is worth fixing even when nothing seems wrong.

Clues that point to a hidden leak behind walls or under floors

If the meter moves and no fixture is leaking, the water may be escaping inside a wall, under a slab or in the yard. A sudden bill jump, a musty smell or a patch of warm floor are the usual clues.

Do not cut into drywall to look. A plumber can listen, scan and trace the leak, and the opening that follows can be a small one. Our leak detection service finds the source without opening more than needed.

  • Meter moves with every fixture off.
  • Bill jumps with no change in habits.
  • Stains, bubbling paint or warped flooring.
  • Sound of running water in a quiet house.

What to do the moment you find water

Shut the main valve first, then open a low faucet to drain the line. If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off those circuits only if you can reach them without standing in water. Move belongings out of the wet area and take photos for your insurer.

If the leak is a burst pipe or active flood, our burst pipe repair team answers the 24/7 line, including nights and weekends, and we book same-day appointments when available.

When a DIY fix is enough and when it is not

Replacing a flapper, tightening a supply connector or changing a washer hose is within reach for many homeowners. Anything that involves cutting pipe, working inside walls or touching the main line is better left to a licensed plumber.

New Jersey licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, and you can verify a plumber through the Division of Consumer Affairs lookup. Permit rules vary, so ask the Carteret construction office or your plumber how a repair will be handled.

Cutting the damage while you wait

Even a small leak can stain drywall and lift flooring within a day. Keep the water off to the affected area if you can, use towels and fans to dry the surface, and move furniture away from damp walls.

Do not use a vacuum on standing water or touch electrical devices near it. If you see sagging ceiling plaster, stay out from under it and call. Mold can begin to grow on damp material, so drying quickly matters more than finishing the diagnosis.

Write down what you did and when, and keep the old meter reading. A plumber can use those notes to estimate how long the leak has been running.

Insurance and documentation

Homeowners insurance generally covers sudden pipe bursts but not slow leaks or wear, so ask your insurer before assuming. Photos with dates, the meter reading and any repair record help your claim.

Tenants should tell the landlord or manager the same day. About 45 percent of Carteret homes are rented, and a written report with a photo protects both sides.

Reading your meter in Carteret after Middlesex Water's street work

Middlesex Water Company supplies Carteret and part of Port Reading. Its RENEW 2023 project replaced about 26,000 feet of main, along with service lines, valves and hydrants, and customers could be without water for six to eight hours during a service line transfer.

The utility is also relocating meters from inside homes to below-ground pits outside over time. If your meter is in a pit, lift the cover carefully and read the dial. If you cannot find it or the dial is unclear, call Middlesex Water at 800-549-3802.

After main work on your street, run cold taps for a few minutes and clean faucet aerators to clear loosened sediment. If the meter still moves with everything off, the leak is on your side of the connection.

Questions readers ask

How can I tell if I have a leak if I cannot find water?

Use the meter test. If the dial or reading changes with every fixture off, water is escaping somewhere, and the toilet and appliance checks help narrow it down.

How much water can a small leak waste?

More than most people expect over weeks. A constant drip or a leaking toilet adds up, which is why a rising bill is often the first sign.

Should I shut off the main water if I find a leak?

Yes, if the water is spraying or flooding, or if you cannot find the source. Know where your main valve is before you need it.

Do you find leaks in Perth Amboy and Fords?

Yes. We serve Perth Amboy and Fords along with Carteret, and the 24/7 line is answered nights, weekends and holidays.

My water bill in Carteret doubled but I see no leak. What now?

Do the meter test. If the meter moves with everything off, call us to trace it. If it does not, compare the bill with your usage and ask the utility to check the meter reading.

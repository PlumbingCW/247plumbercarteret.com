Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Carteret, NJ

Repair or replace the buried pipe between the street main and your house, with leak tracing and shutoff help, any hour.

Call (718) 412-8453See all services

A water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the street main into your house. Water service line repair in Carteret, NJ covers finding a break, fixing or replacing the damaged section and getting pressure back. If water is surfacing in the yard or your supply has failed, call our 24/7 line and close the main valve inside if you can reach it.

Problems on this pipe show up as a wet patch of lawn or sidewalk, a drop in pressure, rusty or cloudy water, or a meter that spins when nothing is running. The pipe is buried and often old, so the first job is to find exactly where it failed before anyone digs.

Signs your service line is failing

A buried line gives warnings that are easy to miss. Pressure that falls slowly over weeks often points to a corroded pipe, while a sudden loss points to a break.

Look for the clues below. If you see more than one, call early, because a small leak under soil can wash out the ground and cause damage that is expensive later.

  • Soggy ground, a sinking patch of lawn or a damp spot on a driveway with no rain.
  • Pressure that has dropped on every tap in the house, not only one fixture.
  • Brown or cloudy water after the pipe has been quiet overnight.
  • A meter that keeps spinning with every fixture off.
  • A water bill that has risen without a change in use.

Water in the yard or no water at all: what to do first

A break in a service line can send a large volume of water into the ground in a short time. When the line is cut off completely, the household has no supply. Both situations are emergencies we take at any hour, including nights and weekends, with same-day visits booked when available.

  • Close the main shutoff inside the house. If the break is before the meter, only the utility can stop the flow at the street.
  • Keep people and pets away from a sinkhole or soft ground over the line.
  • Call 811 before anyone digs, even for a repair, so buried gas and electric lines are marked.
  • Take photos of the wet area and the meter, then call (718) 412-8453.

Middlesex Water, lead lines and the meter move in Carteret

Middlesex Water Company's RENEW 2023 project replaced about 26,000 feet of main in Carteret and part of Port Reading. Street work covered Arthur Avenue, Casey Street, Daniel Street, East Chestnut Street, Marion Street, Sumutka Avenue, Sycamore Street and Tyler Avenue, among others, with new service lines, valves and hydrants.

Under the Knocking Out Lead program, any lead or galvanized steel service line found during the work, including the customer-owned part, is replaced with copper at no direct cost to the owner or tenant. New Jersey requires all utility-owned and customer-owned lead and galvanized lines to be replaced by 2031. That is useful to know before paying for a private replacement on an eligible street.

Meters are also being moved from inside homes to exterior below-ground pits over time, and crews can shut the water for six to eight hours during a transfer. Call Middlesex Water at 800-549-3802 to ask whether your address is in scope, and call us for any failure on the private side.

  • Ask the utility first if your street was on the RENEW list.
  • A free replacement applies only where the program reaches your address, so confirm before authorizing paid work.
  • A leak after the meter is ours to find and fix.

How a service line repair runs from start to finish

Every repair follows the same order, so you know what happens next.

  1. Confirm the leak.

    We read the meter, test the pressure and listen along the route to place the break.

  2. Mark utilities.

    811 is called and the line route is marked so we dig only where it is safe.

  3. Expose and repair.

    We dig a small access pit, cut out the failed section and join new pipe with proper fittings.

  4. Test and restore.

    We pressure test, flush the line, restore water and tell you what to do with the surface.

Finding the break before digging

Digging a long trench to find a leak wastes time and ground. We start with listening equipment on the pipe route and, when the line is dry or isolated, a tracer method that points at the failure from above.

If the break is hard to place, a leak detection survey narrows it to a few feet before any shovel breaks the ground. That keeps the hole small and the lawn, patio or walkway mostly intact.

What drives the cost of a service line repair

Every yard is different, so we state the price after we see the line and before work starts. These factors move it:

  • Length of pipe to replaceA short repair near the foundation is smaller than a run across the whole yard.
  • Depth and soilDeeper lines and tight soil take longer than shallow runs in loose ground.
  • Surface to restoreGrass is simple. Concrete, pavers and driveways add cutting and patching.
  • Pipe materialCopper, lead and galvanized steel each affect how the old pipe is removed and what the new run costs.
  • Emergency timingNights, weekends and holidays are emergency visits and are priced as such, with the number stated first.

Homeowners insurance often excludes damage from wear or corrosion of the line itself, though some policies offer service line coverage as an add-on. Check your policy. For your real price, call (718) 412-8453.

Who owns what, and who to call first

The utility's responsibility ends at the meter or the connection near the street, depending on the arrangement. From there to your house the pipe belongs to the property owner. If you rent, tell your landlord the same day, because the repair is normally theirs to authorize.

In Perth Amboy, the city-owned system is operated by Utility Service Affiliates, a Middlesex Water subsidiary, so the first call for a main-side problem goes there. We can still repair the private line and will say which party owns what.

When the problem is not the service line

Low pressure is not always the buried pipe. A failing pressure reducing valve, a partly closed shutoff or a clogged fixture can all mimic it. We rule those out first, because a trip to dig would be wasted.

If the pressure is fine but a hot tap runs cold, see our water heater repair page. If the wet spot is at a fixture inside, a faucet or toilet fix may be all that is needed. Our crews serve Woodbridge Township as well, where Middlesex Water has run its own RENEW projects.

Code and safety references for water service line repair

Work on the line between the meter and your house follows the plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code, and any digging starts with a call to 811. New Jersey requires all lead and galvanized steel service lines to be replaced by 2031, and Middlesex Water's customer line is 800-549-3802.

Water Service Line Repair questions from Carteret customers

Do you handle water service line repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Carteret?

Yes. The 24/7 line is staffed nights, weekends and holidays. A broken service line floods ground quickly, so close the main valve if you can reach it, call, and we will book a same-day visit when we can.

Who is responsible for the water line from the street to my house?

The utility handles the main and its part of the connection. The pipe from the meter or connection to the house is the owner's. Tenants should notify the landlord right away.

Is lead line replacement free in Carteret?

Middlesex Water says it replaces lead and galvanized steel service lines found during its RENEW work with copper at no direct cost to the owner or tenant, including the customer-owned part. Confirm with the utility whether your address is covered before paying for it yourself.

How long will I be without water during a repair?

It depends on the repair. A spot fix is often done in hours, while a full replacement takes longer. We give you a realistic estimate on site and tell you when water will be restored. If the cause is a split inside the house, see burst pipe repair.

Do I need to call 811 before you dig?

Yes, someone must call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked. We handle that for our own repairs.

Can a leaking service line raise my water bill?

It can. Water lost after the meter is still metered, so a leak between the meter and the house raises the bill, and a meter that spins with every fixture off is the usual sign.

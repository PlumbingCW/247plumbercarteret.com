When Should a Water Heater Be Replaced in Carteret, NJ?

By the Carteret 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026. Updated October 24, 2026.

Replace a tank water heater when it is leaking from the tank itself, when it is roughly a decade old and starting to fail in more than one way, or when repair bills begin to approach the price of a new unit. A leak at a fitting or a dead heating element is usually a repair. A wet floor under the tank, rusty hot water and a loud rumble together almost always mean the tank is on its way out.

This guide helps Carteret, NJ homeowners and landlords tell the two situations apart before an emergency forces the decision. If your heater is flooding the floor right now, shut off the cold water supply to it, turn off the gas valve or the breaker, and call our 24/7 line.

Seven signs the tank is nearing the end

No single sign settles it, but two or three together are a strong signal. Look at the heater itself, not only at how hot the water runs.

  • Moisture or rust streaks at the bottom of the tank, not just at a pipe fitting.
  • Hot water that looks rusty or brown, which points to corrosion inside the tank.
  • Popping or rumbling as it heats, caused by sediment baking on the tank floor.
  • Water that runs lukewarm faster than it used to, because sediment is displacing hot water.
  • A data plate that shows the unit is about ten years old or more, depending on make and model.
  • Repeated repairs within a year, such as a second element, a thermostat and a relief valve.
  • A pilot or burner that keeps failing on a gas unit, which is often a safety-control issue.

A failing tank cannot be patched. Once the steel inner shell leaks, replacement is the only fix.

Why the age of the unit matters so much

Inside every tank heater is a steel shell protected by a sacrificial anode rod. Over the years that rod is consumed, sediment builds up on the bottom and the steel begins to corrode. Many manufacturers rate a standard tank for around a decade, and the data plate on the side of the heater carries a serial number that shows its age, though the format differs by brand.

If you cannot read the date, send us a photo of the plate and we can usually decode it. Knowing the age turns a vague worry into a clear plan, especially when you are deciding whether to spend on a repair.

What to do if the heater is leaking right now

A leaking heater can lose a lot of water quickly, and the damage grows with every minute. Close the cold water valve on the pipe above the heater, switch off the breaker for an electric unit or turn the gas control to off, and put a bucket under the leak.

If you smell gas, do not search for the cause. Leave the building, move at least 350 feet away and call PSE&G at 1-800-880-PSEG (7734) or 911, as PSE&G instructs. Then call us once you are safe. Our water heater repair team handles failed elements, valves and gas controls, and our water heater replacement crews can often swap a failed unit the same day.

Repair costs that add up

A useful rule of thumb is to compare the repair with the age of the heater. A new element on a three-year-old unit is a sensible repair. The same repair on a twelve-year-old heater that is also rusty buys you a short extension and leaves the tank problem in place.

Ask for the repair price and the replacement price together. When the two are close, replacement usually wins because a new unit resets the clock and often uses less fuel.

  • Under five years old: repair almost every time.
  • Five to ten years old: weigh the repair against the likely next failure.
  • Ten years or more: replacement is usually the better value.

Questions to ask before you approve a replacement

A good quote answers the same few questions every time. Ask them of any plumber, including us, so you can compare offers fairly and avoid surprises after the old tank has already been drained.

The answers also tell you whether the plumber has looked at your actual setup or is quoting from a phone description.

  • Is the price for the unit, the labor, the disposal of the old tank and the new fittings, all stated together?
  • Will the expansion tank, relief valve and shutoff valves be replaced or reused?
  • Does the venting or gas line need any change, and who handles the permit paperwork?
  • What is the warranty on the tank and on the labor, and what does it exclude?
  • Permit rules vary, so ask the Carteret construction office or your plumber how the work will be handled.

Should you replace with the same type or switch?

A like-for-like tank swap is the quickest and cheapest path when your system is working. If the old unit was undersized or you are adding a bathroom, a larger tank or a different type is worth considering.

A tankless water heater saves floor space and heats on demand, but it needs the right gas line and venting and carries a higher upfront cost. We explain what your house can support before you decide, and we never push a type that does not fit the plumbing.

What is different about water heaters in Carteret

Carteret's water comes from Middlesex Water Company. In the utility's annual water quality report, system-wide hardness averages about 101 parts per million, roughly six grains per gallon, but individual readings range from 12 to 314 parts per million. That spread means hardness depends on where in the system your water originates, so read your own report before guessing.

Harder water leaves more mineral sediment on the bottom of a tank, which speeds up the rumbling and shortens the time between flushes. If your report or your fixtures show scale, a yearly flush and an anode check can extend the life of the heater you have, and it is worth asking about a water softener when you replace it.

Most Carteret homes with gas heaters get their natural gas from PSE&G, so the gas shutoff steps above apply. In a rental, the unit often belongs to the landlord, so tell the owner or manager as soon as you see a leak and keep a photo of the date and the damage.

Code and safety references for this topic

Replacement follows the National Standard Plumbing Code that New Jersey adopts through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which defines hot water as 120 to 140 degrees Fahrenheit. Permit rules vary, so ask the borough construction office.

Questions readers ask

How long does a water heater last?

Many tank water heaters last around ten years, sometimes longer with regular flushing and anode rod replacement. Tankless units often last longer but need descaling in hard water.

Can a leaking water heater be repaired?

Only if the leak is from a fitting, valve or element gasket. If water is coming from the tank body itself, the steel shell has failed and the unit must be replaced.

Does Carteret's water shorten the life of a water heater?

It can, but it depends on the hardness at your address. Middlesex Water's annual report shows a system-wide range of 12 to 314 parts per million, so check your own report and flush the tank yearly.

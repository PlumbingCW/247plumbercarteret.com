Before we arrive: steps to take while you wait in Woodbridge Township

  1. Find the main valve.

    Locate where the water line comes into the house and close the valve fully. In older townhouses it may sit behind a small access panel.

  2. Protect the electrics.

    Keep clear of outlets and the panel if the floor is wet. Switch off a circuit only if you can reach it from dry ground.

  3. Lift what you can.

    Raise boxes, rugs and electronics off the wet floor and keep children and pets in a dry room.

  4. Call and describe it.

    Dial (718) 412-8453 and tell the dispatcher what is leaking, what you shut off and where the water is going.

How a township this size gets served from one Carteret base

Woodbridge Township is not a single neighborhood. It is a patchwork of communities, each with its own ZIP code and its own housing mix. Woodbridge proper uses 07095, Avenel 07001, Port Reading 07064, Colonia 07067, Sewaren 07077, Iselin 08830, Keasbey 08832 and Fords 08863.

Because the township borders Carteret on all land sides, we do not treat it as a distant service area. The same 24/7 line that answers a Carteret address answers a call from any of these communities. Tell the dispatcher your street and ZIP first, since that is the quickest way to place you.

Flood hazard areas and what they mean for a township basement

About 19 percent of Woodbridge Township sits inside FEMA flood hazard areas, and much of the developed land is only about five feet above sea level. Hurricane Sandy in October 2012 caused major flooding here, and the Watson-Crampton neighborhood was among the hardest hit. The township later used New Jersey Buyout Program funds to purchase and demolish flood-prone homes.

For a homeowner that history has a practical edge. If your block is low, a basement floor drain and a sump pit are the first places water appears, and a pump that fails during a storm turns a nuisance into an emergency. A yearly test of a sump pump is cheap insurance. If water is already rising, call the 24/7 line before the pit overflows.

  • Test the pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit before storm season.
  • Keep the discharge line clear and pointed away from the foundation.
  • Store valuables on shelves, not on a basement floor near the drain.

Water main work across the township and your house lines

Middlesex Water has run its RENEW main replacement program inside the township more than once. The 2022 round covered 24,100 feet of main at a cost of $7.9 million, and the 2025 round covers 22,590 feet at $10.3 million, with restoration planned for spring 2026. New mains mean crews connect each house to a new line, and the water is off for a few hours while that happens.

After a shutoff, air and loose sediment can sit in the pipes. Open the cold taps for a few minutes, then unscrew and rinse the small screens on your faucets. If a fixture still sputters, a faucet repair fixes the cause. A drop in pressure that lasts is a different problem, and our water service line repair team can check the line from the meter.

Sewer backups when the rain is heavy

A backup in the lowest drain of the house usually points to the main sewer line, and heavy rain makes it worse where the ground is low. If only your house is affected, the blockage is on the private side of the property line and a cable or jetter can clear it. Our sewer line cleaning work starts at the nearest cleanout.

If you see no cleanout, say so on the call. Photos help. When a whole street backs up at once, report it to the township as well, because the public sewer may be involved and we cannot touch it.

Is emergency service available in Woodbridge Township?

Is my township basement at risk of flooding?

Possibly. About 19 percent of the township is in FEMA flood hazard areas and Sandy caused major flooding in 2012. Check your address on the FEMA map, test your sump pump each season and keep the floor drain clear.

Why is my water cloudy after Middlesex Water worked on my street?

Main replacement can loosen sediment and trap air in house pipes. Run the cold taps for a few minutes and clean the faucet aerators. If the water stays cloudy or pressure drops, call us and we will check the service line.

Can you work on rental buildings in the township?

Yes. We give the tenant and the owner the same plain account of what failed. For a multi-unit house, tell us which units are affected and we bring the right equipment for a shared line.

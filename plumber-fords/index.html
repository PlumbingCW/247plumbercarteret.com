Before we arrive: stopping a toilet overflow or hidden leak in Fords

  1. Close the fixture valve.

    Turn the small valve behind a toilet or under a sink clockwise to stop the flow. If it sticks, shut the main.

  2. Lift the lid and stop the fill.

    For a toilet that keeps running, remove the tank lid and lift the float so water stops entering the bowl.

  3. Mop up and move things.

    Blot the water, move rugs and keep it from reaching baseboards. Wet drywall dries best when you act early.

  4. Call the 24/7 line.

    Dial (718) 412-8453 and tell the dispatcher what you shut off and whether water is still moving.

Why Fords calls skew toward fixtures and hidden leaks

On high ground the ocean and the tide are not the problem. In Fords the trouble tends to start with the things inside the house: a toilet that will not stop running, a faucet that drips into a vanity, a supply line that weeps behind a wall. These are smaller than a flood, but they cost real money when they run unnoticed.

A running toilet can waste a great deal of water in a day, and the bill shows up weeks later. If your toilet hisses or refills when no one used it, a toilet repair usually fixes the fill valve or the flapper in one visit.

A water bill that jumped: finding a leak you cannot see

A sudden jump in the water bill with no change in habits points to a hidden leak. Shut off every tap and watch the meter. If it still moves, water is going somewhere you cannot see, whether inside a wall, under a slab or on the pipe from the meter to the house.

Our leak detection service finds the source with instruments so we open as little wall as possible. If the leak is outside, a water service line repair may be the answer.

  • Look for damp patches on ceilings and baseboards.
  • Listen for running water when the house is quiet.
  • Check the meter with every fixture off.
